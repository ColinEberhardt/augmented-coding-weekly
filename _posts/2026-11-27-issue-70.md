## [ClawDOS - An AI coding agent for DOS](https://ricapar.net/ClawDOS/)

<small>RICAPAR.NET</small>

The IBM PC XT was a tremendously popular and influential computer. It was launched around 1983 in the same era as the BBC Micro, ZX Spectrum, and Commodore C64, but targeted a different market, establishing the architecture that dominated the business market for decades.  I was lucky enough to have an IBM PC (with monochrome Hercules graphics card) at home, as well as a BBC Micro. While I learnt BASIC on the BBC, I was introduced to Turbo Pascal on the IBM PC. 

ClawDOS is an agent harness that runs natively on this bygone PC, bundled as `HARNESS.EXE`, a 176KB executable application. This harness was, of course, entirely built by Claude; furthermore, the linked blog post was written by Claude too, and you can tell, the phrasing is distinctly AI. Usually I would reject posts that have these obvious AI-smells, but in this case I don't mind at all. It is the idea that matters, and the result is very cool, especially for those of us of a certain age.

![ClawDOS]({{ "img/70.png" | absolute_url }})

The harness has tools, skills, and various compilers and debuggers. It also has the ability to 'see' the screen, allowing it to upload screenshots alongside prompts. Very cool.

## [GLM-5.4: Toward Generalized Coding with Our First Loop Transformer](https://z.ai/blog/glm-5.4)

<small>Z.AI</small>

Zhipu AI (z.ai) is a Beijing-based AI research company that rose to prominence earlier this year with the release of its v5 models, with coding capability close to the frontier (i.e. nearly matching Anthropic and OpenAI). With US-based frontier firms accusing Chinese labs of distillation, using one model to train another, this might give the impression that these labs are just looking to 'clone' the innovations others have created. That couldn't be further from the truth. Z.ai and its peers (DeepSeek and Alibaba) are genuinely innovating, but doing so in a more open way than their US-based counterparts.

In this blog post z.ai share their latest progress on looped transformers. With traditional LLMs, the neural network is organised in layers, with data propagating through the network in a single pass. With looped transformers, each layer runs in a loop, allowing for iterative refinement at each step. The goal is to create a more efficient model, where fewer parameters are needed to achieve the same performance.

## [What does it mean to understand what we built?](https://brooker.co.za/blog/2026/11/16/understanding.html)

<small>BROOKER.CO.ZA</small>

A few weeks ago, a number of speakers and attendees of an AI Engineering conference published the [No AI Slop Manifesto](https://www.slopmanifesto.com/). Emulating the style of the Agile Manifesto of 2001, it describes the values and principles for this new world where AI writes much of the code on our behalf. Importantly, the 'no slop' manifesto prioritises human beings, our time, our attention, and our understanding. I really like it.

However, this blog post pokes at a potential flaw in the manifesto. While it doesn't argue against understanding what we are building (or directing AI to build on our behalf), the manifesto frames understanding at the code level. As we necessarily move our attention away from the minute detail of the code to a higher level of abstraction, perhaps our understanding moves to a higher level also?

> "We’re still looking at each screw, each grain of aggregate, and each PCB trace, and trying to understand the whole by thinking super hard about it. It’s time to look up. And AI gives us an incredible tool to do just that."

I'm still trying to wrap my head around _what_ the author thinks we should be directing our understanding towards. But I agree with the overall observation: the manifesto sits uncomfortably between the old world and a new one we are struggling to describe.
