Augmented Coding Weekly is a short, opinionated weekly curation (typically 3-5 items) focused on how AI is changing software engineering. Each item is a brief commentary (one to four paragraphs) on a single external article, with the author's own voice, experience and mild scepticism woven in. Recurring themes include agentic coding and the "inflection point" in agent capability (Opus 5.5, vibe-coded games, DHH moving Hey to Rust), the second-order effects of abundant code (strain on GitHub and monorepos, Pando/Scion replacing git, open source drowning in bot PRs, AI-discovered vulnerabilities like PHP2Shell, technical debt), and the human side of the shift: taste, judgement, code review, community, whether to learn programming, Brooks' Law with agents.

Sources skew towards personal blogs and essays by known engineers (Simon Willison, Dan Luu, Jeff Atwood, htmx, Emil Kowalski), plus the occasional frontier-lab or company engineering post (OpenAI, Anthropic, GitHub) and the odd video or piece of lighthearted fun (LGTM music video, agent stand-up). Pieces are usually accessible and conceptual rather than deeply technical, though benchmarks and architecture write-ups appear when they carry a wider implication (COBOL benchmark, MCP/Codemode, Pando's design).

The editorial tone is balanced, analytical and hype-free: sceptical of marketing and "death of X" clickbait, but candid about genuinely impressive capability. The author frequently ties items to personal experience, notes weak arguments even in pieces he is covering, and likes tensions and contradictions (open in name but closed in practice, a language disliked by humans but fine for agents). Items about real-world case studies, security/safety of AI tooling, critical reflection on the industry, and tools or techniques for working with agents are favoured over pure model-release news.
